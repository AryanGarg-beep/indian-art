01 · ASSIGNMENTS 1 & 2

Indian Art Timeline & Art Map

From Bhimbetka to Santiniketan, and from Warli to Pattachitra

  • Interactive website
  • HTML, CSS, JavaScript
  • Leaflet

Two linked tools. The timeline places fifteen landmark artifacts across eleven eras; the map pins fifteen living regional traditions to the places that made them.

  • Era filter, arrow-key navigation and a detail view for every artifact
  • Map legend doubles as a category filter, with search by name, state or artist
  • Disputed dates, like the Didarganj Yakshi, are shown as disputed

03 · SUB-ASSIGNMENT 1 · CO2

The Elephant Who Gave His Tusks

A storyboard of the Chhaddanta Jataka, Ajanta Cave 17

  • Storyboard
  • 13-page A4 PDF
  • Illustrated in SVG

The Bodhisattva, born as a six-tusked white elephant, gives up his tusks to the queen who wished him dead. The story is retold in eight illustrated panels and set beside photographs of the original mural.

  • Characters, setting and sequence of events laid out before the panels
  • Every illustration drawn in code as SVG, with the real Cave 17 mural photographed alongside
  • Closes with a historical note on why the story matters at Ajanta

04 · SUB-ASSIGNMENT 2 · CO2

Carving the Cosmos

Arjuna’s Penance and the Pancha Rathas of Mahabalipuram

  • Thematic analysis
  • 7-page report
  • 6 figures

Two 7th-century Pallava works: a cliff face turned into a crowded universe, and five boulders turned into a catalogue of temple forms. Together they show how to tell a sacred story in stone, and how to give a god a house.

  • Zone diagram of the relief and a comparison of the five ratha types
  • Weighs the Arjuna and Bhagiratha readings of the relief
  • Page-numbered A4 layout with references and image credits

05 · SUB-ASSIGNMENT 3 · CO4

Kolam & Rangoli

“Draw the threshold, welcome the world.”

  • Poster
  • A3 portrait
  • Generated patterns

An informative poster on the floor art drawn at India’s doorsteps each morning: what it is called across eight regions, why it is drawn, and how to keep the practice alive.

  • Six labelled pattern tiles, from pulli kolam to a Diwali rangoli
  • Sikku kolams generated by the mirror-curve rule and checked to be one unbroken line
  • A “Did you know?” panel on kolam and mathematics

06 · FIELD VISIT

Field Visit Report

Sri Yoga Hayagrivar Perumal Temple

  • Field report
  • 8 pages
  • Temple architecture

A visit to Sri Yoga Hayagrivar Perumal Temple to study temple art and architecture first-hand, bridging classroom learning with direct observation. The temple’s painted stucco gopuram rises above granite outer walls topped by a brightly coloured cornice.

Visited
14 Nov 2026
Location
Chettippunniyam, Chengalpattu, Tamil Nadu
Report
Introduction, observations, learning outcomes, conclusion, photos, references

What the visit taught

  • Temples worked as centres for art, learning and community life, not only worship
  • How to read the visual and symbolic language of temple carving and architecture
  • Why conserving heritage sites keeps these traditions alive
