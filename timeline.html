Assignment 1

Interactive Timeline with Artifacts

Indian art has never run in a single line. It opens with ochre figures brushed onto rock-shelter walls long before farming, passes to the bronze-casters of the Indus cities and the mirror-polished stone of Ashoka's empire, then gathers into the great religious traditions — the painted caves at Ajanta, the processional bronzes of the Cholas, the manuscript workshops of the Mughal and Pahari courts.

Colonial rule introduced oil paint, the easel and the art school, and with them a long argument about what modern Indian art ought to be. Raja Ravi Varma answered with European technique applied to Indian subjects; the Bengal School answered by rejecting that technique outright; Amrita Sher-Gil answered by refusing the question and looking instead at Ajanta and at village women. The fifteen works below are points along that argument.
