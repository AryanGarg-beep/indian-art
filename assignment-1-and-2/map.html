Interactive Art Map of India

Regional art in India is usually tied to a purpose and a place rather than to a gallery wall. A Warli painting is made on the mud wall of a house for a wedding. A phad is a cloth scroll that works as a portable temple, unrolled at night by a travelling singer who lights each episode with a lamp. A pattachitra stands in for the deities of Puri during the fortnight they are hidden from view.

Most of these traditions were carried by hereditary painter families and sustained by temple or court patronage. Several outlived the demand that created them: Cheriyal scrolls lost their audience to film and television, and Kalighat painting was undercut by cheap printing, though its economy of line went on to shape Jamini Roy. The fifteen forms mapped here run from Adivasi wall painting to the miniature ateliers of the Mughal and Rajput courts, the temple painting of the south and east, and the urban bazaar art of nineteenth-century Calcutta.
